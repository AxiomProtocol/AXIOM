## 3. POSTING STRATEGY

### Frequency
- **Minimum:** 5 posts per week (Monday-Friday)
- **Optimal:** 7 posts per week (daily)
- **Maximum:** 2 posts per day (but only if quality is maintained)
- **Consistency is more important than volume.** The algorithm rewards accounts that post regularly. Missing days resets your momentum.

### Best Posting Times (Eastern/Central)

Your audience checks LinkedIn:
- **Before work:** 7:00-8:30 AM ET (6:00-7:30 AM CT) — commute scrolling
- **Lunch break:** 11:30 AM-1:00 PM ET (10:30 AM-12:00 PM CT)
- **After work:** 5:00-6:30 PM ET (4:00-5:30 PM CT)

**Primary posting window:** 7:15-7:45 AM ET, Tuesday through Thursday
**Secondary:** 12:00 PM ET on Monday and Friday

Tuesday, Wednesday, and Thursday get the highest engagement on LinkedIn. Monday and Friday are lower but still valuable.

### Hook Structure (First 2 Lines)

The first 2 lines appear before the "see more" button. They must create enough tension or curiosity to earn the click. Every click signals the algorithm to show the post to more people.

**Hook formulas that work:**

1. **The Contradiction:** "Everyone says buy index funds. But index funds won't build what your family actually needs."
2. **The Number:** "72 smart contracts. $1M capital target. 0 speculation. Here's how we're doing it."
3. **The Question:** "If 500 families each saved $200/month, what could they own in 3 years?"
4. **The Confession:** "I almost gave up on Axiom last year. Here's what changed."
5. **The Bold Statement:** "The financial system wasn't built for you. So we're building a new one."

### Post Length
- **Sweet spot:** 150-300 words (1,000-1,800 characters)
- **Short posts (under 100 words):** Good for bold statements and contrarian takes
- **Long posts (300-500 words):** Good for stories and educational breakdowns
- **Avoid posts over 500 words** — engagement drops significantly

### Consistency Effects
- **Weeks 1-2:** Algorithm is testing you. Reach will be low (200-500 impressions per post). This is normal.
- **Weeks 3-4:** If you've posted daily, the algorithm starts showing your content to 2nd-degree connections.
- **Weeks 5-8:** Compounding begins. Posts that hit get 2-5x the reach of early posts.
- **Weeks 9-12:** If consistency holds, viral-potential posts start appearing — one post can generate 10,000+ impressions.

**If you stop posting for 3+ days, you lose 2-3 weeks of momentum.** The algorithm deprioritizes inactive accounts quickly.

## 4. LINKEDIN ALGORITHM MECHANICS (2025-2026)

### How Distribution Works

**Step 1 — Initial Test Pool**
When you post, LinkedIn shows it to a small test group (roughly 8-10% of your connections + a sample of followers). This is typically 15-50 people for an account with 154 connections.

**Step 2 — Scoring (First 60 Minutes)**
The algorithm measures engagement signals in the first 60 minutes. This is the "velocity window." Signals are weighted:
- **Comment:** 15x weight (highest value)
- **Share/Repost:** 10x weight
- **"See more" click:** 8x weight
- **Reaction (like, celebrate, etc.):** 1x weight
- **Dwell time (reading without engaging):** 3-5x weight

**Step 3 — Second Wave Distribution**
If the post scores well in the velocity window, LinkedIn pushes it to a larger pool — 2nd-degree connections, topic followers, and people who engage with similar content.

**Step 4 — Extended Distribution (24-72 hours)**
High-performing posts continue getting distributed for up to 72 hours. Some posts see a "second life" on day 2-3 when the algorithm resurfaces them.

### What Kills Reach
- **External links in the post body:** LinkedIn penalizes posts that drive traffic away from the platform. Reach drops 40-60%. Always put links in the first comment, never in the post.
- **Engagement bait:** "Like if you agree" or "Comment YES for..." triggers a spam filter.
- **Editing a post within 10 minutes of publishing:** Resets the velocity window.
- **Tagging more than 5 people:** Seen as spam.
- **Posting more than twice per day:** Your posts compete against each other for your audience's attention.
- **Using LinkedIn's "boost" on organic posts:** Converts organic reach to paid, often reducing total reach.

### Dwell Time
LinkedIn tracks how long someone spends reading your post. Longer posts that hold attention score higher than short posts that get scrolled past. This is why storytelling and line breaks matter — they increase time on post.

**Tactics to increase dwell time:**
- Use single-sentence paragraphs with line breaks between them
- Use numbers and lists (eyes slow down to read structured content)
- Tell stories with tension and payoff
- End with a question that makes people pause and think

### Creator Mode Implications
- Your posts reach followers, not just connections — this is how you grow beyond 500
- The "Follow" button replaces "Connect" — lower friction for new audience
- Your content gets distributed in topic feeds (Real Estate, Blockchain, etc.)
- LinkedIn newsletters become available — powerful for building an owned audience

### Connection Feed vs. Follower Feed
- **Connection feed:** Higher chance of appearing, but limited to mutual connections
- **Follower feed:** Lower per-person probability, but reaches people outside your network
- With creator mode ON, you want to optimize for followers because it gives you access to a much larger addressable audience

## 7. CONTENT FORMATS RANKED BY ALGORITHM PERFORMANCE

### 1. Text-Only Posts (Highest Reach)
**Why:** No external media means LinkedIn doesn't have to load anything — faster rendering = more impressions. The algorithm favors native content. Text posts also generate the highest dwell time when written with strong hooks and storytelling.
**Average reach multiplier:** 1.5-2x compared to image posts
**Best use:** Personal stories, contrarian takes, bold statements, community calls
**Optimal for this profile:** 3-4 times per week

### 2. Carousel/Document Posts (PDF Uploads)
**Why:** Swipeable content keeps people on-platform longer. Each swipe counts as engagement. LinkedIn treats document posts as high-value content.
**Average reach multiplier:** 1.3-1.8x
**Best use:** Educational breakdowns, step-by-step guides, data visualizations
**Optimal for this profile:** 1-2 times per week
**Tip:** Create simple 5-8 slide carousels using Canva. Use large text, minimal design, one idea per slide.

### 3. Native Video (Under 90 Seconds)
**Why:** LinkedIn is pushing video hard in 2025-2026. Native video (uploaded directly, not YouTube links) gets preferential treatment.
**Average reach multiplier:** 1.2-1.5x (but highly variable)
**Best use:** Quick takes, behind-the-scenes, explaining a concept face-to-camera
**Optimal for this profile:** 1 time per week
**Tip:** Vertical format, captions on, first 3 seconds must hook. Keep under 90 seconds.

### 4. Image + Text Posts
**Why:** Solid performance but not as strong as pure text. Images can increase dwell time if they're informative (charts, screenshots) but decrease it if they're stock photos.
**Average reach multiplier:** 1.0x (baseline)
**Best use:** Platform screenshots, data/metrics, event photos, infographics
**Optimal for this profile:** 1-2 times per week
**Never use:** Stock photos, generic motivational quote images

### 5. Polls
**Why:** High engagement rate (clicking a poll option is low friction). But LinkedIn has reduced poll distribution after overuse. Use sparingly.
**Average reach multiplier:** 0.8-1.2x
**Best use:** Market research, audience preferences, sparking debate
**Optimal for this profile:** 1 per month maximum

### 6. LinkedIn Newsletters
**Why:** Subscribers get notified by email and push notification. This is the most reliable distribution channel on LinkedIn.
**Average reach multiplier:** N/A (direct to subscribers)
**Best use:** Weekly or bi-weekly deep dives on community wealth, asset ownership, Axiom updates
**Optimal for this profile:** Start one in Month 2. Aim for 500+ subscribers by Month 3.

### 7. Long-Form Articles
**Why:** Lower algorithmic reach than posts, but higher perceived authority. Good for SEO and for linking from your website.
**Average reach multiplier:** 0.5-0.7x
**Best use:** Deep thought leadership pieces, case studies, whitepapers-lite
**Optimal for this profile:** 1 per month

### 8. Reposts with Commentary
**Why:** Lowest reach of all formats. LinkedIn wants original content, not reshares.
**Average reach multiplier:** 0.3-0.5x
**Best use:** Only when adding substantial commentary (3+ sentences) to someone else's viral post
**Optimal for this profile:** Avoid unless adding real value

## 12. COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

### 1. Leading with Crypto/Blockchain Jargon
**Why it hurts:** Your target audience is skeptical of crypto. Words like "Arbitrum," "DeFi," "tokenized SPVs," and "smart contracts" in your headline or opening lines will make them scroll past. They think "scam" before they think "opportunity."
**Do instead:** Lead with outcomes (own real assets, build wealth together) and explain the technology in the body of your posts using plain English.

### 2. Posting Links in the Body of Your Post
**Why it hurts:** LinkedIn reduces distribution by 40-60% when you include external links. The algorithm wants to keep users on-platform.
**Do instead:** Put your link in the first comment. Write "Link in comments" or "Link in first comment" at the end of your post.

### 3. Not Responding to Comments in the First Hour
**Why it hurts:** The algorithm measures engagement velocity in the first 60 minutes. Every reply you write counts as additional engagement on your post, boosting distribution.
**Do instead:** Block 30-45 minutes after posting to reply to every single comment. Your replies should be substantive (2+ sentences), not just "Thanks!"

### 4. Treating LinkedIn Like Twitter/X
**Why it hurts:** LinkedIn rewards depth, nuance, and professionalism. Hot takes without substance, political rants, and confrontational tone get suppressed.
**Do instead:** Every contrarian take should include evidence, personal experience, or data. Disagree respectfully. Build bridges.

### 5. Using Stock Photos or Generic Motivational Quotes
**Why it hurts:** LinkedIn users scroll past these instantly. Zero dwell time = algorithm death.
**Do instead:** Use real screenshots of your platform, real photos from events or meetings, real data charts. Authenticity beats polish.

### 6. Inconsistent Posting Schedule
**Why it hurts:** The algorithm rewards consistency over everything. Posting 7 days straight then disappearing for a week is worse than posting 3 days every week consistently.
**Do instead:** Batch-create content on weekends. Write 5 posts on Sunday, schedule them throughout the week. Use LinkedIn's native scheduling feature.

### 7. Sending Blank Connection Requests
**Why it hurts:** Acceptance rate drops to 20-25% without a message. With a personalized note, acceptance rates hit 40-50%. More connections = more reach.
**Do instead:** Always include a connection message (see templates in Section 6).

### 8. Talking About Your Product Too Much
**Why it hurts:** LinkedIn penalizes overly promotional content. If every post is "Check out Axiom Protocol," people unfollow and the algorithm suppresses you.
**Do instead:** Follow the 80/20 rule. 80% of posts should educate, inspire, or tell stories. 20% can mention Axiom directly. The other 80% should still implicitly build interest in what you're doing.

### 9. Ignoring DMs
**Why it hurts:** DMs are where followers become community members, investors, or partners. LinkedIn also tracks DM activity as an engagement signal.
**Do instead:** Respond to every DM within 24 hours. Even a short reply builds relationship. Use DMs to convert engaged commenters into deeper relationships.

### 10. Not Using Creator Mode
**Why it hurts:** Without Creator Mode, your content only reaches connections. With 154 connections, that's a tiny pool. Creator Mode unlocks follower-based distribution.
**Do instead:** Turn on Creator Mode immediately (see Section 1).

### 11. Posting Only During Business Hours Eastern
**Why it hurts:** Your Houston/Charlotte audience is in Central time. Posting only at ET peak hours misses the CT morning window.
**Do instead:** Alternate posting times between ET and CT peaks throughout the week.

### 12. Writing for Industry Insiders Instead of Your Audience
**Why it hurts:** Your target audience is W-2 professionals, not DeFi developers or VCs. Content written for insiders alienates the people you're trying to reach.
**Do instead:** Before publishing every post, ask: "Would a 32-year-old nurse in Houston understand and care about this?" If not, rewrite it.

### 13. Not Completing Your Profile Sections
**Why it hurts:** LinkedIn search ranks complete profiles higher. Missing sections (experience, education, skills, volunteer work) reduce your visibility in search results.
**Do instead:** Fill out every section. Add your University of Phoenix education. Add volunteer work. Add certifications. The more complete, the higher you rank.

### 14. Ignoring Analytics
**Why it hurts:** Without checking what's working, you'll keep making the same mistakes and miss opportunities to double down on winners.
**Do instead:** Every Sunday, review your LinkedIn analytics. Identify your top 3 posts by impressions. Ask why they worked. Create more like them.

### 15. Expecting Overnight Results
**Why it hurts:** Most people quit in weeks 2-3 when engagement is still low. The compounding effect doesn't kick in until weeks 5-8.
**Do instead:** Commit to 90 days minimum. Track weekly trends, not daily numbers. The growth curve is exponential, not linear — it looks flat at first, then explodes.
