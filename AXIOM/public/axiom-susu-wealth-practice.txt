PART 1 — TRADITIONAL SUSU CIRCLES

========================================================================
1. WHAT IS A SUSU?
========================================================================

A SUSU (also known as a tontine, chit fund, tandas, or rotating
savings and credit association — ROSCA) is one of the oldest
financial instruments in human history. It predates modern banking
by centuries.

At its core, a SUSU is simple: A group of people agree to
contribute a fixed amount of money at regular intervals. Each
period, the entire pot goes to one member. The rotation continues
until every member has received the pot once. Then the cycle is
complete.

Example:
  10 members contribute $100/week
  Total weekly pot: $1,000
  Each week, one member receives $1,000
  After 10 weeks, every member has contributed $1,000 and
  received $1,000
  No interest. No bank. No middleman. Just trust.

The SUSU is not a loan. No one pays interest. No one profits from
lending. It is a commitment device — a structured way for people
to save money they might otherwise spend, and to access a lump
sum they could not accumulate alone as quickly.

========================================================================
2. HOW TRADITIONAL SUSU WORKS
========================================================================

FORMATION:
  A trusted organizer (sometimes called the "banker" or "hand")
  recruits members, usually from family, church, workplace, or
  neighborhood. Members know each other personally.

AGREEMENT:
  Members agree on:
  - Contribution amount (e.g., $50/week)
  - Contribution frequency (weekly, bi-weekly, monthly)
  - Rotation order (who gets paid first, second, etc.)
  - Total number of members (determines cycle length)

EXECUTION:
  Each period, every member contributes their agreed amount.
  The full collected pot goes to that period's designated
  recipient. This continues until every member has received
  the pot exactly once.

COMPLETION:
  When the last member receives their payout, the cycle is
  complete. Members may choose to start a new cycle or
  disband.

ROLES:
  Organizer — Collects contributions, manages schedule,
  handles disputes. Sometimes receives a small fee or gets
  first/last position.

  Members — Contribute on time, receive their payout when
  their turn arrives, honor the commitment through the
  full cycle.

========================================================================
3. THE CULTURAL LEGACY
========================================================================

SUSU circles are not a niche practice. They are a global
tradition spanning continents and centuries:

  West Africa:    "SUSU" or "Esusu" (Yoruba origin)
  Caribbean:      "Sou-Sou" or "Meeting Turn"
  South America:  "Tandas" (Mexico), "Juntas" (Peru)
  South Asia:     "Chit Funds" (India), "Committees" (Pakistan)
  East Asia:      "Hui" (China), "Tanomoshi" (Japan), "Kye" (Korea)
  Middle East:    "Gameya" (Egypt), "Sanduk" (Sudan)
  Europe:         "Tontines" (historical France and Italy)

These circles have historically served communities excluded
from formal banking — immigrants, diaspora communities,
unbanked populations, and working-class neighborhoods.
They represent collective self-reliance: communities banking
themselves when institutions would not serve them.

The SUSU is the original community bank.

4. RISKS OF TRADITIONAL SUSU
========================================================================

Traditional SUSU circles carry significant risks. Understanding
these risks is essential to appreciating what The Wealth Practice solves.

RISK 1 — DEFAULT RISK (Member Stops Paying)
  The single greatest risk. After a member receives their payout,
  they have reduced financial incentive to keep contributing.
  A member who receives early in the cycle and stops paying
  leaves later members short.

  Impact: Later-position members lose money. The circle collapses.
  Frequency: Common, especially in larger or loosely organized groups.

RISK 2 — ORGANIZER FRAUD
  The organizer collects and handles all the money. If the
  organizer disappears with funds or mismanages collections,
  the entire circle fails.

  Impact: Total loss for all members.
  Frequency: Rare in tight-knit communities, more common as
  groups scale beyond personal relationships.

RISK 3 — NO TRANSPARENCY
  Members often have no visibility into total collections,
  who has paid, or the current balance. They rely entirely
  on the organizer's word.

  Impact: Disputes, accusations, broken relationships.
  Frequency: Very common, especially in cash-based circles.

RISK 4 — NO LEGAL RECOURSE
  Traditional SUSU circles are informal agreements. There is
  no contract, no court that will enforce contributions, and
  no way to recover funds from a defaulting member.

  Impact: No recovery mechanism for victims.
  Frequency: Universal — this is structural to the model.

RISK 5 — TRUST FRAGILITY
  The entire system depends on personal trust. One bad
  experience (a default, a late payment, an argument)
  can destroy trust not just in that circle but in the
  concept itself for all participants.

  Impact: Emotional damage, community fractures.
  Frequency: Common, and generational — families pass down
  negative SUSU experiences.

RISK 6 — LATE PAYMENT DISRUPTION
  Even when members intend to pay, late payments disrupt the
  entire cycle. If contributions are not collected on time,
  the recipient cannot receive their full payout.

  Impact: Delayed payouts, frustration, cascading tardiness.
  Frequency: Very common.

RISK 7 — POSITION INEQUALITY
  Members who receive early in the rotation get more value
  (they receive a lump sum before contributing the full
  amount). Members who receive last have essentially saved
  the full amount before receiving their payout — they get
  no benefit from the time value of money.

  Impact: Perceived unfairness, disputes over rotation order.
  Frequency: Structural to every SUSU.

RISK 8 — NO INSURANCE OR PROTECTION
  If a member defaults, there is no backup fund, no insurance,
  and no mechanism to make other members whole.

  Impact: Losses absorbed entirely by remaining members.
  Frequency: Structural — no traditional SUSU has insurance.

RISK 9 — SCALING LIMITATIONS
  Traditional SUSU works well with 5-15 people who know each
  other personally. Beyond that, trust breaks down and
  coordination becomes unmanageable.

  Impact: Cannot grow to serve larger communities.
  Frequency: Universal limitation.

RISK 10 — NO RECORD-KEEPING
  Cash-based circles leave no paper trail. There is no way
  to prove participation, build credit, or demonstrate
  financial discipline from SUSU activity.

  Impact: Zero benefit to creditworthiness despite consistent
  savings behavior.
  Frequency: Universal — the financial system does not
  recognize SUSU participation.

NOTE: Axiom Protocol has rebranded its implementation of the
SUSU model as "The Wealth Practice." The traditional term
"SUSU" is preserved throughout Part 1 for historical and
cultural accuracy. In Parts 2 and 3, "The Wealth Practice"
refers to Axiom's on-chain implementation of these principles.

5. WHAT IS THE WEALTH PRACTICE?
========================================================================

The Wealth Practice takes the ancient wisdom of rotating savings
circles (traditionally known as SUSU) and rebuilds it on blockchain
infrastructure. The core principle remains: community members save
together and take turns receiving a lump sum. But every traditional
weakness is addressed through automated control layers, on-chain
transparency, and built-in financial protections.

The Wealth Practice is not a bank. It does not lend money. It does
not charge interest on savings. It is a structured savings framework
governed by automated control layers that enforce the rules no
human organizer can be trusted to enforce perfectly.

The philosophy is simple: Same ancient wisdom. Modern
accountability. No trust required — only verification.

7. HOW THE WEALTH PRACTICE MANAGES TRADITIONAL RISKS
========================================================================

Each of the 10 traditional SUSU risks has a specific Wealth
Practice solution:

RISK 1 — DEFAULT RISK
  Traditional: Member stops paying after receiving payout.
  Wealth Practice Solution: Automated control layers enforce
  contribution schedules. Members cannot withdraw or skip without
  the contract registering a default. Defaulting members are
  flagged on-chain, their AxiomScore is reduced, and the Insurance
  Fund covers the shortfall for remaining members.

RISK 2 — ORGANIZER FRAUD
  Traditional: Organizer disappears with collected funds.
  Wealth Practice Solution: There is no human organizer holding
  funds. The automated control layer is the organizer. Funds are
  held in either the pooled contract (WealthPracticeHub) or
  individual vaults (PersonalVault). No single person has
  withdrawal access. The code is the custodian.

RISK 3 — NO TRANSPARENCY
  Traditional: No visibility into who paid or current balance.
  Wealth Practice Solution: Every contribution, every payout, every default
  is recorded on Arbitrum One. Any member can verify the state of
  their circle at any time by reading the contract. Transaction
  history is permanent and public.

RISK 4 — NO LEGAL RECOURSE
  Traditional: No enforceable contract, no recovery mechanism.
  Wealth Practice Solution: The automated control layer IS the contract.
  It is self-enforcing. Contributions are programmatic — the
  code does not forgive, forget, or play favorites. Rules are
  executed exactly as written, with no human interpretation
  or discretion.

RISK 5 — TRUST FRAGILITY
  Traditional: One bad experience destroys trust in the system.
  Wealth Practice Solution: Trust is replaced by verification. Members
  do not need to trust each other or an organizer — they verify
  the contract code, the on-chain state, and the insurance fund
  balance. Trust is earned through AxiomScore, not assumed.

RISK 6 — LATE PAYMENT DISRUPTION
  Traditional: Late payments delay the entire cycle.
  Wealth Practice Solution: The contract enforces grace periods
  (configurable per circle). After the grace period expires,
  automatic late fees are assessed. Chronic lateness reduces
  AxiomScore. The system creates strong economic incentives
  for on-time contributions.

RISK 7 — POSITION INEQUALITY
  Traditional: Early recipients get more time-value benefit.
  Wealth Practice Solution: Because contributions are in AXUSD (a
  stablecoin pegged 1:1 to USD), there is no inflation
  erosion. Additionally, the protocol can implement
  interest-offset mechanisms where later-position members
  receive slightly larger payouts to compensate for time
  value, funded by protocol fees.

RISK 8 — NO INSURANCE
  Traditional: Defaults absorbed entirely by members.
  Wealth Practice Solution: The InsuranceFund (funded by 5% of
  DePIN node rewards) exists specifically to cover defaults.
  If a member defaults, remaining members are made whole
  from the insurance pool. Higher fund levels mean higher
  protection — this is displayed as a coverage metric on
  each circle's dashboard.

RISK 9 — SCALING LIMITATIONS
  Traditional: Cannot grow beyond 5-15 trusted individuals.
  Wealth Practice Solution: Because trust is replaced by automated
  enforcement and insurance, circles can scale to larger
  sizes. The three-stage trust pipeline (Purpose Groups to
  Wealth Practice Circles to full Wealth Practice) allows strangers to
  build trust progressively before committing funds.
  Regional hubs organize members by geography and interest.

RISK 10 — NO RECORD-KEEPING
  Traditional: No credit benefit from SUSU participation.
  Wealth Practice Solution: AxiomScore. Every on-time contribution,
  every completed cycle, every act of reliability is recorded
  on-chain and reflected in a score from 300 to 850. This
  score unlocks access to larger circles, lower collateral
  requirements, and eventually, investment opportunities
  through The Wealth Practice.

8. AXIOMSCORE: ON-CHAIN REPUTATION
========================================================================

AxiomScore is an on-chain reputation system that functions
like a credit score — but built on what you actually DO,
not on debt you take on.

Range: 300 to 850 (mirrors traditional credit scoring scale)

Score Inputs:
  - Contribution consistency (on-time payments)
  - Completed circle cycles (full rotations finished)
  - Community engagement (helping new members, vouching)
  - Payment streak length (consecutive on-time payments)
  - Default history (defaults reduce score significantly)
  - Circle size and duration (larger/longer = more weight)

What AxiomScore Unlocks:
  300-499: Entry-level circles ($25-50/week, small groups)
  500-649: Mid-tier circles ($50-100/week, medium groups)
  650-749: Premium circles ($100-250/week, larger groups)
  750-850: Wealth Practice eligibility, Capital Mode access,
           investment pool participation, reduced collateral

Key Difference from Traditional Credit:
  - Built on savings behavior, not borrowing behavior
  - Cannot be manipulated by corporations
  - Stored on-chain — portable, verifiable, owned by you
  - No credit inquiries, no hidden algorithms
  - Transparent scoring formula

Your AxiomScore is your financial autobiography written
on-chain. Every on-time payment is a chapter. Every completed
cycle is a milestone.

9. THE WEALTH PRACTICE INSURANCE FUND
========================================================================

Contract: 0x7B69ce0d83f45C2dBa3e5B73076beA8b1Be1271F

The Insurance Fund is the safety net that makes The Wealth Practice
fundamentally different from traditional SUSU.

Funding Source:
  5% of all DePIN node rewards (DeNet Datakeeper nodes)
  are automatically routed to the Insurance Fund.

Purpose:
  When a member defaults on their contributions, the
  Insurance Fund covers the shortfall so remaining circle
  members receive their full expected payouts.

How It Works:
  1. A member misses contributions beyond the grace period
  2. The contract flags the member as defaulted
  3. The defaulting member's AxiomScore is reduced
  4. The Insurance Fund pays the difference to the circle
  5. Remaining members are made whole
  6. The defaulting member cannot join new circles until
     their score recovers through a rehabilitation path

Coverage Metrics:
  Each circle displays its insurance coverage level.
  Higher fund balances mean higher coverage ratios.
  Members can assess the protection level before joining.

This is the mechanism that eliminates the greatest fear of
traditional SUSU: "What if someone stops paying?"

Answer: The Insurance Fund pays. You are protected.

10. AXUSD-DENOMINATED CIRCLES
========================================================================

Adapter: 0x4c17360651c2c46F1739E92f512D8ce6318106b4

Traditional SUSU circles using volatile currencies or even
cash face a hidden risk: the value of contributions can
change between when you contribute and when you receive
your payout.

Wealth Practice circles denominated in AXUSD eliminate this risk:

  - AXUSD maintains a 1:1 peg to USD
  - Contributions are in stable dollar value
  - Payouts are in stable dollar value
  - No inflation erosion during the cycle
  - Automatic PSM conversion for members holding USDC

If you contribute $100/week for 10 weeks, you receive $1,000.
Not "$1,000 worth of something that might be $900 tomorrow."
Exactly $1,000.

11. THE THREE-STAGE TRUST PIPELINE
========================================================================

The Wealth Practice does not start with money. It starts with people.

The system is designed so that financial commitment follows
human connection. Members build credibility before joining an
on-chain circle. This is the Three-Stage Trust Pipeline:

  Stage 1: PURPOSE GROUPS (Trust Building)
    No money involved. Members find each other, align on
    goals, exchange vouches, and build reputation.

  Stage 2: WEALTH PRACTICE CIRCLES (Savings Execution)
    On-chain circle with automated enforcement. Members
    contribute, rotate, and build AxiomScore.

  Stage 3: THE WEALTH PRACTICE (Investment Graduation)
    After proving reliability through completed savings cycles,
    groups qualify to expand into larger investment vehicles
    including real estate, infrastructure, and capital pools.

Each stage has graduation requirements. You cannot skip stages.
Trust is earned, not assumed.

========================================================================
12. STAGE 1: PURPOSE GROUPS
========================================================================

Purpose Groups are the entry point. No financial commitment
is required. This is where strangers become allies.

How It Works:
  1. Join a Regional Hub (organized by geography: city,
     state, region, or nationwide)
  2. Browse Purpose Categories (savings goals, real estate
     interest, education funding, emergency preparedness)
  3. Join or create a Purpose Group aligned with your goal
  4. Begin building relationships with other members

Activities in Purpose Groups:
  - Introduction and goal-sharing
  - Financial literacy discussions
  - Vouching for other members you trust
  - Attending group check-ins
  - Learning about Wealth Practice mechanics
  - Building AxiomScore through engagement

Graduation Requirements (Purpose Group to Wealth Practice Circle):
  - Minimum member count met
  - Members have exchanged mutual vouches
  - Group has been active for minimum qualifying period
  - All members have completed onboarding education
  - Group creator initiates graduation when thresholds are met

No money changes hands during Stage 1. This stage is
purely about human trust, alignment, and education.

Database Infrastructure:
  susu_interest_hubs — Regional hub registry
  susu_purpose_categories — Goal categories
  susu_purpose_groups — Individual groups with graduation tracking
  susu_hub_members — Hub membership and roles
  susu_group_members — Group membership and roles

13. STAGE 2: WEALTH PRACTICE CIRCLES (ON-CHAIN)
========================================================================

Once a Purpose Group meets its graduation requirements, it
transitions to an on-chain Wealth Practice circle.

This is where money enters the picture — but only after
trust has been established in Stage 1.

Circle Configuration:
  - Contribution amount (e.g., $25/week, $50/week, $100/week)
  - Contribution frequency (weekly, bi-weekly, monthly)
  - Number of members (determines cycle length)
  - Custody model selection:
      Pooled Custody (WealthPracticeHub) — Contributions pooled in
      shared contract, payouts distributed automatically.
      Self-Custody (PersonalVault) — Each member's
      funds stay in their own vault until payout assembly.

On-Chain Mechanics:
  - Automated contribution collection per schedule
  - Grace period for late payments (configurable)
  - Late fee assessment after grace period expires
  - Automatic rotation of payouts to next recipient
  - Real-time state viewable by any member on-chain
  - Protocol fee (small percentage routed to treasury)
  - Insurance Fund coverage for member defaults
  - AxiomScore updates after each contribution event

What Members Experience:
  Week 1: Everyone contributes $100. Member A receives $1,000.
  Week 2: Everyone contributes $100. Member B receives $1,000.
  ...
  Week 10: Everyone contributes $100. Member J receives $1,000.
  Cycle complete. Every member saved $1,000 and received $1,000.
  AxiomScores updated. Readiness for next stage assessed.

Basic Readiness Tracking:
  The system tracks each circle's readiness metrics:
  - Member count vs minimum threshold
  - Contribution consistency percentage
  - Total pot value
  - Completed cycles count
  - Overall readiness score (0-100%)

14. STAGE 3: THE WEALTH PRACTICE
========================================================================

The Wealth Practice is the destination. It is where disciplined
savers become collective investors.

After a Wealth Practice circle completes multiple successful
rotations and meets Capital Mode thresholds, it qualifies to graduate
into The Wealth Practice — a larger investment vehicle where
the group can pool resources for opportunities that no single
member could access alone.

The Wealth Practice is not a new circle. It is an expansion
of a proven circle into a collective investment unit.

What Changes at Graduation:
  - Group can accept new members (expanding beyond original size)
  - Group can pool capital for investment opportunities
  - Members gain governance voting rights on investment decisions
  - Access to Axiom's real estate pipeline, lending fund,
    and infrastructure investment opportunities
  - Enhanced AxiomScore weighting
  - Eligibility for larger capital commitments

What Does NOT Change:
  - Contribution discipline remains mandatory
  - On-chain transparency continues
  - Insurance Fund coverage continues
  - AxiomScore tracking continues
  - Automated control layers enforce all rules

15. WHAT GRADUATION MEANS
========================================================================

Graduation is not automatic. It is earned through demonstrated
reliability.

Graduation from Wealth Practice circle to full Wealth Practice requires:

  Option A — Time-Based:
    6+ months of active participation with all members in
    good standing (no defaults, no chronic late payments)

  Option B — Cycle-Based:
    3+ successfully completed full rotation cycles with
    all members in good standing

  Option C — Capital Mode Thresholds:
    Meeting any combination of capital mode metrics:
    - Minimum total capital contributed across all cycles
    - Minimum number of completed cycles
    - Minimum member count
    - Minimum average AxiomScore across all members

When Graduation is Ready:
  1. The system displays "Ready to Graduate" on the group
     dashboard with a readiness score of 100%
  2. The group creator initiates the graduation transaction
  3. An on-chain graduation event is recorded
     (graduation_tx_hash stored permanently)
  4. The group transitions from Community Mode to Capital Mode
  5. A new charter is created on-chain formalizing the
     investment group
  6. The group ID is linked to a graduated pool ID for
     tracking lineage

Estimated Graduation Timeline:
  Conservative: 6-9 months (monthly circles, 3 cycles)
  Moderate: 4-6 months (bi-weekly circles, 3 cycles)
  Aggressive: 3-4 months (weekly circles, 3 cycles)

16. CAPITAL MODE: FROM SAVER TO INVESTOR
========================================================================

Capital Mode is unlocked upon graduation. This is where the
transition from saver to investor happens.

Community Mode (Pre-Graduation):
  - Traditional savings circle mechanics
  - Fixed contribution, rotating payout
  - Focus: Discipline, savings, trust-building
  - Ceiling: Limited to circle size and contribution level

Capital Mode (Post-Graduation):
  - Collective investment vehicle
  - Pooled capital for investment opportunities
  - Focus: Potential returns, asset acquisition, wealth-building discipline
  - Ceiling: Expandable — new qualified members can join

The Wealth Ladder:
  Level 1 — SPENDER: All money goes out
  Level 2 — SAVER: You keep some, but it sits
  Level 3 — INVESTOR: Your money works for you
  Level 4 — OWNER: Assets generate income

Wealth Practice circles take you from Level 1 or 2 to Level 2 or 3.
The full Wealth Practice takes you from Level 3 to Level 4.

What Capital Mode Groups Can Do:
  - Vote on investment proposals as a collective unit
  - Pool contributions toward specific investment targets
  - Access Axiom Lending Fund opportunities (SEC Reg D 506(c))
  - Participate in real estate asset onboarding pipeline
  - Invest in DePIN infrastructure nodes
  - Access Euler V2 lending market yields
  - Contribute to Axiom Capital Program positions

Every investment decision is made by group governance vote.
No single member, including the original organizer, can
unilaterally deploy group capital.

18. THE FULL JOURNEY: $25/WEEK TO PROPERTY OWNERSHIP
========================================================================

Here is the reference architecture from first contribution to
collective property ownership:

MONTH 1-2: PURPOSE GROUP
  Join a regional hub. Find your people. Learn about The Wealth Practice.
  Exchange vouches. Build initial AxiomScore through
  engagement. No money required.

MONTH 3-5: FIRST WEALTH PRACTICE CIRCLE
  Start small — $25/week with 8 members.
  Total pot per rotation: $200.
  Complete your first full cycle (8 weeks).
  AxiomScore begins climbing with every on-time payment.
  Insurance Fund provides default protection.

MONTH 6-8: SECOND AND THIRD CYCLES
  Increase to $50/week. Prove consistency.
  Complete second and third cycles.

RISK DISCLOSURE
========================================================================

This document describes a framework that combines financial
technology with community participation. The following risks
should be understood by all participants:

1. Automated control layer risk: All contracts are
   source-verified on Arbiscan but have not undergone an
   independent third-party security audit. OpenZeppelin
   standards are used throughout.

2. Insurance Fund limitations: The Wealth Practice Insurance Fund
   coverage depends on DePIN node revenue. If node revenue
   is insufficient, the fund may not fully cover all defaults.

3. Real estate risk: Property investments carry market risk,
   liquidity risk, and platform risk. Fractional real estate
   tokens may not be easily sold. Property values can decline.

4. Regulatory risk: The legal treatment of rotating savings
   circles, fractional real estate ownership, and on-chain
   investment pools may vary by jurisdiction. Participants
   should consult qualified legal counsel.

5. AxiomScore limitations: AxiomScore reflects on-chain
   behavior within the Axiom ecosystem only. It is not a
   substitute for traditional credit scoring and may not be
   recognized by external financial institutions.

6. Bootstrap phase: Axiom Protocol is in early bootstrap
   (Week 5 of 52-week playbook). Many features described
   in this document are deployed but not yet activated.
   SEED and Wealth Practice activation is planned for
   Weeks 11-13.

7. No guaranteed returns: Past performance is not indicative
   of future results. No yield, return, or appreciation is
   guaranteed. All investment outcomes depend on market
   conditions, property performance, and group governance
   decisions.

GLOSSARY
========================================================================

Automated control layers:   Self-executing code on the blockchain
                            that enforces rules without human
                            intervention (commonly called "smart
                            contracts" in technical contexts)

Multi-party authorization:  Requiring multiple approved parties
                            to confirm a transaction before it
                            executes (commonly called "multi-sig")

On-chain financial rails:   Financial infrastructure built on
                            blockchain networks (commonly called
                            "DeFi" in technical contexts)

Asset onboarding:           The process of representing real-world
                            assets as on-chain instruments (commonly
                            called "tokenization" in technical
                            contexts)

Participation lockup:       Locking tokens in a contract to earn
                            rewards and governance rights (commonly
                            called "staking" in technical contexts)
